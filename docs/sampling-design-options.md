## Candidate nested unit structure

The following structure is recommended for adviser review:

1. **Institution** — primary sampling and recruitment unit. Authorization,
   participation, withdrawal, and institution-level inclusion are recorded
   here.
2. **Campus** — nested operational unit when a framework or source
   distinguishes main and satellite campuses, particularly for
   campus-dependent sustainability evidence. Campus records must retain their
   parent institution and must not create duplicate institution participants.
3. **Institution-framework assessment** — an institution-by-framework unit
   used to record eligibility, required data, evidence completeness, and
   assessment status. One institution may be eligible for some frameworks and
   not others.
4. **WURI program/case** — a nested program-level unit for WURI submissions.
   A WURI program is not an institution, campus, or institution-wide ranking
   observation.

This structure is consistent with the documented framework differences, but
the primary sampling unit and nesting rules remain a proposed design awaiting
researcher/adviser approval.

## Design options

### Option A — Stratified probability sample

**Procedure:** Freeze and reconcile a complete accessible CHED frame, define
the institution as the primary unit, assign predeclared strata, and select
institutions using a documented probability procedure. Recruit selected
institutions and report non-response and missing evidence.

**Possible strata:** region, public/private or other official sector,
institution type, and framework eligibility. Strata must be available and
reliable in the frozen frame; they must not be invented from participation
outcomes.

**Strengths:** Best basis among these options for describing selection
probabilities and limited population generalization if the frame is complete
and recruitment is followed.

**Risks:** The current frame is not frozen or known complete. Authorization
requirements may create non-response and coverage bias after selection.
Frameworks have different eligibility and campus/program units.

**Dissertation compatibility:** The dissertation explicitly says purposive
sampling for expert and system-evaluator respondents (Section 3.3.3), but
does not explicitly prescribe probability sampling for HEI evidence. Adviser
approval and a protocol amendment or clarification would be required.

**Approval needed:** Frame completeness, strata, selection algorithm, sample
size/precision rationale, non-response and replacement rules, and
generalization claim.

### Option B — Purposive sample of eligible, authorized institutions

**Procedure:** Define explicit eligibility and evidence requirements, invite
institutions meeting the approved criteria, and include those that provide
usable authorized evidence under the approved recruitment process.

**Possible criteria:** institution-level participation consent; framework
eligibility; ability to provide required evidence; campus/program
identification; and authorization, research-use, privacy, ethics, and
retention clearance.

**Strengths:** Directly matches the dissertation's stated purposive rationale
for specialized participants and the practical requirement for authorized
evidence. It is feasible when access is constrained.

**Risks:** Strong self-selection and access bias; the participating
institutions cannot automatically represent all Philippine HEIs. Institutions
unable to provide evidence would be excluded from the empirical corpus, not
silently treated as negative or zero readiness.

**Dissertation compatibility:** Compatible with Section 3.3.3's purposive
sampling language, but the dissertation does not specify that this method
applies to institutional evidence units. That scope requires adviser
confirmation.

**Approval needed:** Eligible population, explicit criteria, target sample
size rationale, recruitment, non-response, withdrawal, replacement, and
limits on generalization.

### Option C — Stratified purposive sample

**Procedure:** Freeze a candidate frame, define meaningful region/sector/type
and framework-eligibility strata, set a feasibility-based target or minimum
representation for each approved stratum, and purposively recruit eligible
institutions within strata.

**Strengths:** Preserves purposive access and authorization requirements while
making coverage targets visible. It can reduce an obviously narrow
participant mix.

**Risks:** It does not remove selection bias or create probability-based
inference. Small or inaccessible strata may be unrepresented. Quotas chosen
without a defensible frame or rationale can create false balance.

**Dissertation compatibility:** Plausibly compatible with the dissertation's
purposive approach, but the institutional strata and allocation are not
specified in Chapter 3 and require approval.

**Approval needed:** Frame and strata definitions, allocation rule, minimum
representation, sample size rationale, recruitment and replacement policy,
and generalization boundary.

## Leading proposal for adviser review

For the recorded mixed platform/research design, **Option C — stratified
purposive sampling** is the leading proposal for adviser review. This is not
an approved final method. It should only be finalized after the candidate
CHED frame is reconciled and the researcher approves the strata, recruitment
rules, and sample-size rationale.

The proposal has the following review constraints:

- **Population:** Philippine HEIs, excluding elementary and secondary
  schools.
- **Institution types:** public and private HEIs, subject to verified
  eligibility and evidence availability.
- **Regions:** consider representation across Philippine regions, with final
  strata deferred until the CHED frame is reviewed.
- **Framework eligibility:** assess eligibility separately for WURI 2026, THE
  World University Rankings 2026, QS World University Rankings 2026, and UI
  GreenMetric 2025.
- **Participation:** include only institutions meeting approved criteria and
  providing authorized, usable evidence.
- **Method:** stratified purposive sampling, explicitly proposed and not
  approved.
- **Sample size:** unresolved until objectives, accessible population,
  recruitment feasibility, and analysis requirements justify a number.
- **Limitations:** report non-response, selection bias, and limits on
  generalizing to all Philippine HEIs.

These constraints do not assume universal participation or qualification for
all four frameworks. The final strata, eligibility rules, sample size, and
replacement policy remain on the approval checklist.

## Sample-size rationale required before selection

The researcher must distinguish at least:

- number of participating institutions;
- number of institution-framework assessments;
- number of campuses;
- number of documents and evidence passages;
- number of WURI programs/cases;
- number of expert ratings;
- number of system evaluators (the dissertation states at least 30 for
  software-quality evaluation).

The institutional target must be justified from the study objective,
accessible eligible population, intended estimand/generalization, expected
recruitment and non-response, framework coverage, and any applicable
precision or power analysis. No arbitrary institution count is entered here.

## Recruitment and non-response proposal

The eventual protocol should define invitation, information/consent,
institutional authorization, framework eligibility screening, evidence
completeness screening, withdrawal, missing criteria, and retention/deletion
procedures. Institutions that cannot provide authorized evidence should be
reported as non-participants or incomplete cases according to the approved
rule; they must not be assigned fabricated evidence or scores.

Replacement is unresolved. If allowed, the protocol must predefine the
replacement pool and report original selection, refusal, replacement, and
potential selection bias. If not allowed, the final sample must report
non-response and its limits.
